## 3. Adverse Reaction / Event

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| Reaction / Event | Nausea, watery diarrhoea (4–5 episodes/day), abdominal discomfort, mild fatigue |
| Onset | 3 days after metformin dose was increased to twice daily |
| Course | Persisted 2 days; metformin reduced back to once daily on day 3 by attending physician |
| Outcome after Dechallenge | Diarrhoea reduced to 1 episode/day within 48 hours; nausea resolved |
| Rechallenge | Not attempted — dose increase deferred |

## 4. Causality Assessment (WHO-UMC Scale)

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Time Relationship | Plausible — symptoms began 3 days after dose escalation |
| Dechallenge Response | Positive — clear improvement after dose reduction |
| Rechallenge Response | Not done |
| Known ADR Profile | GI upset is a well-documented, dose-dependent effect of metformin |
| Alternative Explanations | None identified — no new infection, diet change, or other new drug; concomitant medicines unchanged |
| **Causality Category** | **Probable / Likely** |

## 5. Assessor's Notes

This case demonstrates a **Probable** ADR to metformin, supported by a plausible timeline and positive dechallenge response, with no more likely alternative cause. A rechallenge, if clinically appropriate, would allow upgrading the causality category to *Certain*. Recommend documenting as a Probable ADR in the UMTH pharmacovigilance log and reporting to NAFDAC/WHO-UMC via the national ADR reporting channel.
